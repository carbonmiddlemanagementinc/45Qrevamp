A three-tier concentration-based credit structure: below 30% CO₂ (hard industrial), above 30% CO₂ (high-concentration), and direct air capture. The 30% threshold separates sectors where capture requires genuine post-combustion separation work from those producing near-pure CO₂ streams as a process byproduct.

How the simulation works
Deployment

Each sector in the Rhodium/GPI universe from Tab 01 has a capture cost range. In each entry year, the share of a sector that is bankable is the Tab 01 midpoint rule applied to the effective credit. Eight percent of the bankable-but-unbuilt potential comes online each year from 2029. Coal is discounted to 25% and refineries to 50% of potential for barriers unrelated to the credit. Existing operations are excluded and unaffected.

Learning

Capture cost falls with cumulative tier deployment on an experience curve. Central rates: Tier 1 10%, Tier 2 3%, DAC 15% per doubling (low 5/1/8, high 15/5/20), from an assumed experience base of 20, 25 and 1 Mt/yr. Point-source rates follow analog technologies in Rubin et al. (2007); DAC follows Young et al. (2023). Costs bottom out at 65%, 90% and 40% of today's.

Credit period and financing

Costs are levelized over the credit period at an 8% real cost of capital, with capital at 55% (Tier 1), 35% (Tier 2) and 60% (DAC) of cost. A 12-year period reproduces Tab 01. Extending it spreads capital recovery over more years, which lowers the credit a project needs. Credited tonnes count only within the credit period.

Indexing

All results assume 2.3% general inflation. "None" lets the statutory amount erode in real terms. "General" holds it constant in real terms, as current law does. "Plant-cost" adds 1%/yr real, matching the assumed escalation of capital costs (Chemical Engineering Plant Cost Index against the GDP deflator).

Fiscal window

10-year outlays are nominal credits claimed in 2027–2036 at 85% utilization, the window a JCT score would see. Full-life outlays add every year of every 2027–2040 vintage's credit period in real 2026 dollars. Neither is a JCT or CBO score. Treat as ±50%, and read differences between designs, not levels.

Not comparable to Tab 01

Tab 01 scores 19 named projects. This tab simulates the whole facility universe, so its levels are larger. Its purpose is to compare designs under one consistent set of assumptions.
